## Controlled object and naming

Use `W[out,in]`, conventional up factor `B[out,r]`, conventional down factor `A[r,in]`, adapter multiplier `s`. The accepted local DoRA forward is `V=W+BA`, `W_eff=m*V/||V||column` (axis 0), full derivative through the denominator, ambient norm arithmetic, and dense projection. Its implicit `s=1`, frozen BF16 base, FP32 factors and magnitude, random-up/zero-down initialization are retained as the main control. This differs from published standard LoRA's random-down/zero-up convention. Distribution and orientation must not be conflated.

The principal initializer experiment keeps both factors trainable, rank and total parameter count fixed, and preserves the starting function. For a nonzero product, the runtime uses `V=W+(BA-B0A0)` with identical autocast for the two products. This is a **compensation representation adaptation** of papers storing `Wr=W-sB0A0`, not a claim of bitwise equivalence to their low-precision residual storage. Initial weights, outputs, and denominator/magnitude behavior must pass the same parity gate. Literal residual storage is a separate numerical diagnostic. Warm-start methods also get separately labeled arms; compensating their intended initial gradient/domain-shift step changes the published method.

`experiments/init_data.py` generates CPU FP32 factors from a weight, rank, plain dictionary spec, and optional calibration statistics. It does not alter W, magnitude, model state, global RNG, optimizer, rank allocation, or factor trainability. The runner controls compensation. `adapter_scale` is explicit; accepted-runtime experiments set it to 1. A common reciprocal factor balance `B*=g; A/=g` is a local conditioning ablation, not a paper default.

## Random, noise shape, and kernel controls

[LoRA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685) initializes random down and zero up; [initialization asymmetry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08447) motivates separately controlling orientation. [Nonzero initialization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23194) supplies both-nonzero and compensated variants: author code samples A and B uniformly with bounds `mag_A/sqrt(in)` and `mag_B/sqrt(in)` (both use original input width). [NoRA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31036) supplies one-time normalized down columns and BIMI (repeated rank-sized identity blocks, partial final block), both with zero up. Repeated identity columns are not globally mutually orthogonal. Per-forward NoRA changes the training parameterization and belongs to the already separate study.

PEFT's `orthogonal` initializer is distinct from OLoRA: even rank, one random rank×rank orthogonal Q, complementary odd/even row subspaces, `A=(Gaussian[in,r/2] Qodd).T/10`, `B=Gaussian[r/2,out].T Qeven/10`; mathematically BA=0 with both factors nonzero. This initially has factor ranks at most r/2. BF16 numerical identity still needs verification.

Designed controls, not attributed to papers: variance-matched Gaussian, uniform, Rademacher, Laplace, Student-t (nu>2), truncated Gaussian, sparse Rademacher (Bernoulli times a random sign); Haar/QR, normalized spherical factors, signed/permuted BIMI; RBF `exp(-d²/(2ell²))`, exponential/AR `exp(-abs(d)/ell)`, Matern3/2, shared-mode covariance, DCT/Hadamard and block structure. Compare one-zero orientations separately. Preserve explicit RMS when changing shape and log tails, maximum absolute value, covariance spectrum and effective rank. Coordinate index has no inherent spatial order, so smooth kernels need permutation controls. Gains {.25,.5,1,2} are relative to an explicit RMS baseline, not ambiguous initializer names.

## Scope boundaries and additional discovered work

- [LoftQ](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08659) (`experiments/init_loftq.py`) uses the pinned PEFT NF4 default: 4 bits, flat contiguous blocks of 64, FP32 absolute-max scales, no double quantization, one iteration by default. The dense reference codebook is checked against bitsandbytes 0.49.2 installed in an isolated study directory. It alternates quantization of `W-BA` and low-rank approximation of the quantization residual. It belongs to a controlled quantization sub-study with the same quantizer-only baseline; it cannot be presented as a pure initializer over the unchanged BF16 base. Preserve bits, codebook, block size, iteration count and reconstruction residual. Initial identity is generally approximate. A dense dequantized simulation measures initialization quality, not compressed serving speed/memory.
- [MiCA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01694) uses minor left singular vectors with zero other factor but freezes a factor. A train-both minor-subspace initializer is executable, but full MiCA is a constraint change.
- [LoRA-SB](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19557) trains a middle matrix between two fixed bases; [LoRA-Pro](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.18242) changes per-step gradients. Neither is a two-factor initializer-only experiment.
- Gap-Init (modality-gap rank-one correction), GME-Init (geometry of audio task activations), and extreme-space/subspace papers were discovered as contextual leads, not independently verified executable recipes here. They require primary-source review before an exact-method claim. Their specialized task statistics are not silently synthesized from unrelated calibration. This finite slate can be extended after those sources resolve, rather than claiming unbounded literature completeness.

## Calibration, candidate fairness, and numerical gates

Calibration uses training split IDs only, fixed before validation. Record exact IDs, tokenizer/padding/attention and label masks, counts of examples/tokens/batches, task loss and its reduction, and gradient scaling. No validation/test labels or activations enter any initializer. Gradient-derived factors refer to the base model full-weight loss gradient; their adaptation to DoRA's normalized update geometry is explicit. All methods share the same task/calibration opportunity unless their original protocol requires separate knowledge data, in which case label the task-only derivative.

Required per-module statistics: `input_cov=E[xx.T]`, `input_centered_cov`, `input_mean`, `input_count`; `output_cov`, optional `astra_output_cov` following source batch normalization; `weight_grad`; `output_grad_cov=E[dy dy.T]`; `output_fisher_diag=E[dy²]`; `training_population_size` for LoRA-DA (the distinct calibration example count is not N); optional raw `input_gram_batches` for CNTLoRA. Squared weight-gradient diagonal is not a substitute for FILet's output-gradient covariance or DA's output Fisher diagonal. Covariance naming refers to uncentered moments unless `centered` is explicit. Shared pooled/masked statistics are a calibration-protocol adaptation where paper preprocessing differs.

Proposed balanced pilot opportunity is four initializer settings × two common task learning rates per family, with deterministic tie-breaking and reserved independent final seeds. This is a candidate proposal; the runner's frozen registry is authoritative. For spectral methods settings can vary reciprocal balance; for GA/One gamma; for DA beta/gamma; for CorDA damping/mode; for EVA whitening/scale; for IniLoRA prefit budget/scale. Do not let a large family silently receive many more validation attempts. Report family maxima with candidate count and uncertainty, not only one lucky seed.

Before task fits: verify finite shapes, rank constraints, local RNG isolation, unmodified W/statistics, no trainable parameter-count change, reconstruction/formula residuals, CPU small-matrix reference agreement, compensated initial forward parity under actual BF16 autocast, and first-step gradient flow. Cache factors with source/spec/calibration hashes and timing. Runtime report includes calibration, decomposition/prefit, train and evaluation costs separately and end-to-end. Failed initialization gates remain explicit failed candidates. Long candidate fits do not replace completing the final multiseed comparison.

IniLoRA prefit can run in FP32 on the assigned CUDA device (`prefit_device`) with CPU-seeded initial draws and CPU FP32 returned factors. Its device, TF32 policy, iterations and timing are recorded. Projection-group standard deviations may be supplied in calibration metadata (`inilora_group_std`); missing exact group statistics must not silently become a paper-default claim. LoRA-DA source collects moments in train mode, while the common evaluation-mode calibration is a documented protocol adaptation.

`init_data.py` now selects `corda_input_cov` and `tlora_input_cov` when present, otherwise records a pooled-moment adaptation. Factor, decomposition and IniLoRA prefit caches bind CPU thread count because dense solver factors can differ bitwise across thread counts. The prefit cache excludes final gain/balance settings, reuses the actual source U[out,r]/P[in,r] Gaussian draw layout, and verifies content hashes. Intentional source warm starts are available in the separate `init_warmstart.py` backend; their downstream quality comparisons are still outstanding.
